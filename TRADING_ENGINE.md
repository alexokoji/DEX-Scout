# Trading engine

## Signal -> trade
1. Signal engine emits BUY/WATCH from an `Analysis` (score, safety, trend). Tokens with critical safety issues never get signals.
2. **Manual**: `POST /api/trades/quote` (read-only) -> `/prepare` (server validates, stores a PREPARED trade, 60s TTL) -> `/execute` (id only; amounts/prices come from the stored trade).
3. **Auto**: `bot.ts` walks active BUY signals by score: user filters, re-weighted score, capital allocation, fresh analysis, quote, `validateEntry(automatic)`, sell simulation, then queues the trade for wallet approval.

## Validation (`core/trading/validation.ts`)
Quote expiry, critical safety issues, sell simulation, price impact, slippage cap, min liquidity/volume, non-zero output; automatic entries also need min score and max risk level.

## Capital (`core/trading/capital.ts`)
Capital is the connected wallet's balance — there is no typed-in trading capital. `allocate()` clamps to max position, available capital (the wallet's native balance on the chain being traded, further limited by the optional max-deployed cap) and open-position slots; rejects below min position. An unknown balance (no wallet linked, RPC down) is not treated as zero.

**Fees and affordability come from the chain, not from numbers we chose.** Nothing is estimated and padded: no amount is held back from a wallet and there is no margin on any fee. Whether a wallet can afford a swap is the chain's answer, given when the swap is prepared, with its real figures in the message. Solana: the node simulates the exact transaction as the wallet will send it (`simulateTransaction`), and says how much the wallet has and how much the swap needs. EVM: the node's own gas estimate (`eth_estimateGas`) for the real transaction from the real wallet, times the current gas price, set against the wallet's balance (`affordability.ts`); a swap that would revert is caught with the node's own reason, and what the balance alone proves (the wallet can't send more than it holds, a wallet holding none can't pay a fee) is refused even when a node words its error unrecognisably. A sell needs an approval first, so the swap can't be estimated until it exists: the approval is estimated and the aggregator's own gas figure for the swap is added, and the wallet checks the swap when it sends it. A node that doesn't answer is "unknown", never "not enough". The balance shown and used for limits is the whole native balance. Fees in a quote: Solana's base fee is `getFeeForMessage`, and its priority fee is the going price per compute unit in the fee market of the pools the route trades through (`getRecentPrioritizationFees`) times the compute units Jupiter sizes for that exact swap and wallet (its swap response's `computeUnitLimit`; the build asks once to learn the units and again with the fee priced for them), with no built-in ceiling, only the user's own cap; EVM: the aggregator's own gas figure for the route at the chain's gas price (measured for the same small swap: 711,585 gas on Base from one aggregator, 194,640 from another, so no constant is right). A fee that can't be read is unknown (`networkFeeKnown: false`, "your wallet shows the exact fee"), never invented. The per-chain `mockFeeUsd` / `mockNativeUsd` and `FEES` exist only for the mock market; a test checks the live code doesn't mention them. Jupiter's smallest limit order and its fee are not kept either: the order builder is asked, and its refusal ("Order size must be at least 5 USD, received: 4.84") gives the minimum, scaled by how its measure of our order compared with ours; its fee comes out of each fill and is shown from the fill. What is still a choice and can't come from a chain: how high in the Solana fee market to bid (`LANDING_QUANTILE`, the 90th percentile: the chain says what others pay, not how much of a rush to be in), and the lifetime given to CoW and Kyber orders (14 days). Not included in EVM estimates: the L1 data fee on OP-stack chains (Base, Optimism, Unichain...), which `eth_estimateGas` leaves out; the wallet shows the exact total. SOL and every other native price is read from the market, with no hard-coded fallback. The capital check runs inside `withUserLock` (a real MongoDB multi-document transaction, auto-retried on conflict) so concurrent workers cannot over-allocate.

## Solana token-account deposits (`core/providers/solana/tokenAccounts.ts`, `reclaim.ts`)
A Solana wallet holds each token in its own token account, and the chain keeps a deposit locked in every account for as long as it exists. The first buy of a token opens its account and locks that deposit (about 0.0015 SOL, which can be more than a small trade); selling empties the account but does not close it, so the deposit stays locked until the empty account is closed, which returns all of it. It is not a fee and nobody takes it, but it leaves the wallet, which is why a wallet can end a profitable round trip lower than it started.

- **Before the buy**: the quote reads what the new account will lock from the chain (the built swap is run and the new account's balance is read; the chain's own rent-exempt minimum is the fallback; 0 when the wallet already holds the token; unknown is shown as unknown) and shows it with the total that leaves the wallet now, warning when it is more than the trade.
- **After the sale**: Positions and Wallet show "Deposits you can get back" for the wallet's empty token accounts (each checked against the chain first). One signature closes them (several per transaction) and returns the deposits; once confirmed the refund is recorded against the position whose buy opened the account.
- **What the wallet really did**: each confirmed trade stores what it did to the wallet's native balance, read from the transaction (`walletChange`: balance change, fee, deposit locked or returned). A position shows "Your wallet" next to the price-based profit: swaps, fees and deposit held add up to the wallet's total. The profit figure itself stays the price change only. Shown only when every transaction of the position was read from the chain.

## Profit taking (`core/trading/targets.ts`)
**Every position has its own targets.** The ladder in Settings is only the default a new position starts with. A position's own ladder (`targetsSnapshot`) is what the monitor and the auto-sell orders use, so one position can aim at +8% and another at +20%. It is set three ways: typed in the trade panel when you buy (stored with the trade and becoming the position's ladder when the buy confirms), changed afterwards on the position (Positions → Targets → edit; `POST /api/positions/[id]/targets`), or, if you choose it in Settings, drawn for each bot buy from that token's own history (falling back to your ladder when there isn't enough). Changing a position's targets re-plans what is left from now: back to its first target, each target selling its share of what is held now, gains still measured from the entry price, proceeds already booked kept, the change recorded in the position's history. It is refused while auto-sell is armed (the orders sit with the venue at the old targets: cancel auto-sell first); suggested orders are discarded and rebuilt when armed.

Each target sells a share of the position's amount at the time the ladder was set; the last sells the remainder. Gaps across several targets fire each in order. Only price **gain** is evaluated.

**Projected rise (`core/analysis/projection.ts`).** The analysis says what to expect from a token, from its own price history: for every moment in its recent 15-minute candles (about ten days), how far did the price climb at its best within the next hour, four hours, a day? The spread of those answers is the base rate for that token ("within 4h it reached +1.5% half the time, +2.6% a quarter of the time, +3.9% one time in ten, and typically dipped -1.4% first"), and every target in the editor shows how often the token's own history reached it. It is a base rate, not a forecast: it assumes the token keeps behaving as it has lately, says how much history it rests on (a window with few separate stretches behind it is flagged, and a window with too little history isn't shown), and no figure in it is chosen by us. Measured live: BONK, WIF, BRETT and PEPE rose only +1% to +2% within 4 hours half the time, and an +8% target was reached in about 0-2% of their 4-hour windows and 5-18% of their daily ones, which is exactly what a flat +8% for every position hides. The presets (cautious / typical / ambitious) pick points on the token's own curve (targets it reached in 80%-40%, 60%-15% or 35%-5% of past windows) and keep the sell shares from the default ladder; they use the longest window with at least five separate stretches of history behind it. Signals draw their price targets the same way (null when there isn't enough history), instead of the same three percentages for every token.
## Unattended trading (`services/autonomous.ts`, `services/botWallet.ts`, `core/trading/governor.ts`)
The bot can trade with no signature per trade, using a **bot wallet**: a separate wallet whose key the server holds (one Solana address and one EVM address that covers every EVM chain), funded by the user with only what they accept to risk. Its secret is sealed with AES-256-GCM under `BOT_WALLET_KEY`; it can be emptied only to the user's own verified wallet; its key can be exported (password-checked). Switching it on (Unattended page) puts the bot in LIVE and running; switching it off pauses the bot (it does not fall back to approvals). Open positions are never touched by either: they keep their sell targets.

**Signing.** A trade prepared for the bot wallet goes through the same `prepareTrade` / `refreshPreparedTrade` as any other (limits, trust, quote, affordability, a fresh quote just before signing), then `executeBotTrade` takes a claim on it (so it is signed at most once), signs it, **records the signature before sending** (so a transaction that lands is never untracked), and sends it; booking is the normal `reconcileLiveTrade`. EVM: a token approval is sent only when the aggregator's contract can't already move the amount (this also removes the approval that every manual EVM sale used to include), then the swap once the approval is mined (a slow approval resumes on the next cycle without a second one). The EVM transaction's nonce, gas price and gas limit come from the chain (the gas limit has 20% headroom over the node's estimate; unused gas is refunded). A trade that can't be completed is failed; a failed sale is reported to the user.

**Positions in the bot wallet** are sold by the server: at their targets (the monitor), on emergency exits if enabled, and when the user presses Sell. They need no auto-sell limit orders and never appear in the approval queue. Capital limits (open positions, deployed amount, balance) count only the bot wallet's own exposure and balance, including buys still landing.

**Scalping.** Entries use scalp-sized targets: the token's own shortest projected window (one hour) at the cautious preset (rises reached in most of its past windows), with the user's sell shares. An entry is skipped when the quote's fees and price impact, in and out, would eat the first target (`core/trading/scalp.ts`): small positions on expensive chains aren't opened. Exits are decided each monitor cycle (about a minute), so a target is taken when the price is seen at or above it, not at a tick.

**Token-account deposits.** On Solana every token the bot buys opens a token account that locks a deposit, and a sale leaves the empty account behind. Once a position of the bot wallet has closed, the monitor has the bot close its empty token accounts itself and the deposits come back (it looks only when a position has closed since the last time).

**The daily governor** (`evaluateDay`) decides whether the bot may OPEN a trade today; it only ever limits risk, because a bot told to chase a target trades harder the further behind it is. The day (UTC, or shifted by the offset) is added up from the bot wallet's confirmed trades, net of the fees it really paid (sales' profit or loss less fees; buys' fees). States: *chasing* the target; *above target* (reached: it keeps taking profits and may go beyond, with a floor under the day: the day's peak less the give-back share of the target); *target locked* (profit fell back to the floor: stops for the day with most of the target banked; give-back 0 = stop at the target); *loss limit* (banked result plus what open positions are down reaches the limit: no new trades until tomorrow; open gains don't offset it, they aren't banked); *cooldown* (N losing closes in a row pauses for a while). Stopping never sells anything: existing positions keep their targets. The user is notified once per state per day.

## How much a sale asks for (`core/trading/sellAmount.ts`)
A position's token amount is a number built from the chain's integer balance, and a double can't hold an 18-decimal balance exactly. Converted back to whole units for a swap it could come out a few hundred units above what the wallet holds (6 of 10 open EVM positions were over when checked, by 0.8 to 4.6 billion units), and the token contract refuses to move more than the balance, which a wallet shows as "insufficient balance" on the swap prompt that follows the approval. A sell quote now reads the wallet's real token balance and never asks for more; selling the whole position (or within the float's own precision of it) sells exactly the balance. Applies to EVM (every aggregator) and Solana.
## When a target is reached but the order hasn't filled
An armed auto-sell is a limit order, not a market sell: it fills only when a buyer takes it at its price, so the market touching the target doesn't guarantee a fill (a small order on a quiet chain can wait, or never fill). The position monitor therefore compares the market price with each armed EVM order's target: if the price is at or above it on two checks in a row (the first only marks the order; dropping back clears the mark) and the order still hasn't filled, it queues a sell for the user to sign ("Target N reached, but its auto-sell order hasn't filled"). Solana orders are left alone: their tokens sit in escrow, so they can't be sold another way while the order stands. A Solana position below Jupiter's minimum order size has no order at all, so reaching a target only queues a sell for the user to sign (valid for 10 minutes): only the wallet can sign, so nothing can sell it while the user is away.

The monitor reads a position, spends seconds on market data, then writes its status back. It no longer writes to a position that closed in the meantime (a sale booked while it was running used to be undone, leaving a sold position in the open list); any already caught that way are put right when positions are listed and at the start of each monitor cycle.
## No stop loss
There is no code path that sells because a position is negative. `assessPosition` deliberately has no price/PnL input. A losing position is `OPEN` (health `HOLD`/`MONITOR`/`WARNING` informs, never sells).

## Emergency protection (separate, opt-in)
Triggers only on: token untradeable/no market data, pool inactive, sell simulation failing, liquidity down >= threshold since entry (default 70%), other critical security condition, extreme exit price impact (>=40%). With *Emergency detection* on you get warnings; with *Allow automatic emergency exit* on a full exit is queued for wallet approval.
Max position age only closes positions that are **in profit**; losers are held with an event note.

## Position status
`OPEN`, `TARGET_1..3`, `PROFITABLE`, `EMERGENCY`, `CLOSED`. Health: `HOLD`, `MONITOR`, `WARNING`, `EMERGENCY`.

## Backtesting
`core/strategy/engine.ts` replays bars through the same target logic, reporting P/L, drawdown, win rate, holding period and capital utilisation. It does not prove future profitability.

## Live path
Every trade is LIVE — there is no simulated/paper mode. `prepareTrade` builds an unsigned transaction (Jupiter on Solana, 0x on EVM); the user's wallet signs and sends; `/execute` records the signature; `reconcileLiveTrade` follows it on-chain and only then creates/updates the Position. The bot cannot sign for your own wallet, so it holds no withdrawal authority over it. Live position exits are queued the same way. (Unattended trading is the exception, with a separate bot wallet: see the section below.)
## Notifications
Everything is recorded in the in-app bell (header; also a toast and, if allowed, a browser notification while the app is open) and pushed to the user's optional free channels — an ntfy.sh topic and/or a Discord webhook (Settings → Notifications). Hosts are fixed server-side, so a saved value can't make the server call an arbitrary URL. Delivery never throws into the trade flow. Wording lives in `services/notificationMessages.ts`.

| Category (switchable) | Events |
|---|---|
| Waiting for your signature | a buy the bot queued (`prepareTrade` AUTO_ENTRY); a target / emergency / max-age sell (`prepareLiveSell`); a bot-queued trade that expired unsigned (`expirePreparedTrades`) |
| Trade results | a buy or sell confirmed on-chain (with realised P/L on sells); a trade that failed, timed out or didn't match the expected swap (`reconcileLiveTrade`) |
| Position alerts | a position's health turning WARNING or EMERGENCY (`monitorPosition`) |
| System problems | no scan for 20 minutes (`checkScannerHealth`, run by the monitor job so it works when the scan job is the thing that died; sent to users with a linked wallet) |

Repeats are suppressed per key (a sell that keeps re-queuing, a flapping position, the same token queued again, the scanner outage) with a reminder window of 30 min–6 h. Emergencies go to ntfy at urgent priority. Hand-made buys/sells you sign right away don't notify. Old notifications are pruned after 30 days.

## Token trust (`core/analysis/trust.ts`, `core/providers/trust.ts`)

Passing the old safety screen only meant "no red flag was detected", and a brand-new token with every authority revoked passes it, so scams passed too (measured: a token with a revoked mint and freeze authority, 58 holders, $5K of liquidity and an organic-trading score of 0). Tokens now have to **earn trust** from independent checks plus real depth and history. Five tiers, worst to best: **Dangerous** (honeypot, confirmed rug, a tax of 15% or more), **Risky** (a named red flag), **Unproven** (nothing wrong found, but not enough to trust yet, or the checks couldn't be run), **Trusted**, **Verified** (Trusted and on a curated list someone else maintains).

- **Where the facts come from (no API keys):** Solana: Jupiter's token API (verified list, organic score, dev holding) and RugCheck (rug and danger flags, liquidity locked or burned, creator share). EVM: GoPlus (honeypot, taxes, contract powers, source published, LP locks, trusted list) and honeypot.is (a real simulated buy and sell with the real taxes). Each service says only what it knows; a field it can't speak to stays unknown, and unknown is never a pass. Results are cached 15 minutes (90 seconds if the main service didn't answer), a rate-limited service is left alone for 2 minutes, and nothing here can throw: with no service reachable a token is simply Unproven.
- **The bar for Trusted** (all in `TRUST_BAR`): $50K liquidity, $20K 24h volume, 24h of history, 100 holders (when known), a Jupiter organic score of 30+, a real sell test or an unfrozen token, locked or burned liquidity (70%+) unless the pool is $250K+ and a week old, no tax above 5%, no hidden owner or mint/proxy/pause powers (EVM), mint and freeze revoked (Solana), and no danger flags. Verified skips history and holder count and the admin-power rules for tokens on a curated list (CAKE can mint, USDC can blacklist), but never the liquidity floor, taxes, rugs or hidden-owner checks.
- **Calibrated against live tokens.** BONK, WIF and JUP read 14%, 48% and 1% "locked" (concentrated-liquidity pools can't be locked) and RugCheck rates "Single holder ownership" a danger on every one of them (the pool vault), so those are cautions, not flags. A copycat "Bonk" with $1.7M of liquidity, 85 holders and $130 of daily volume is caught (freeze authority on, "Copycat token").
- **What it gates:** the bot buys nothing below the user's *Minimum trust* setting (default Trusted); signals are raised only for Trusted+ tokens; a Dangerous token can't be bought by anyone; a hand-made buy of an Unproven or Risky token needs the buyer to tick "I understand" (enforced on the server too: `acknowledgeTrust`). The Scanner and Tokens pages list Trusted+ tokens by default, say how many were left out, and can show everything. Each token page shows the check-by-check reasons.
- **Chains no service covers.** honeypot.is simulates sells only on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Base (measured), so requiring its simulation left every token on every other EVM chain at Unproven forever, and the default Trusted+ list hid whole chains. GoPlus's own buy/sell test (in a pool, not a honeypot, with a measured sell tax) now counts as a sell test on every chain GoPlus covers, an empty tax reads as unknown rather than 0%, and a curated list vouches for a sell test or tax the service couldn't measure. A chain that no service covers at all (GoPlus's own supported-chains list, read live, is the authority; currently HyperEVM and Ink among ours) can't produce a Trusted token, so its tokens stay listed under a trust floor, labelled "Can't be verified", and the bot still won't buy them. The "hidden" banner says how many tokens each chain lost to the trust floor, with a link to each.
- **Liquidity:** the default minimums moved from $20K/$10K to $50K/$20K (settings v5; anyone still on the old defaults moves, a value chosen on purpose stays).
- A pass is not a guarantee. Anyone can still dump, and a clean contract can lose value fast.
## Auto-sell (sell by itself when a target is reached)
The bot never holds the keys of your own wallet, so it cannot sell from it for you. What it can do is prepare the sells in advance and let a keeper network execute them on-chain. When a buy confirms, `suggestAutoSells` prepares one limit sell per remaining profit target (the same amounts the manual target logic would sell) and notifies you. On the Positions page one wallet interaction **arms** them; from then on each sells on-chain when its price is reached, even if you're away. Nothing sells by itself until you arm it, and you can cancel at any time.

| Venue | Chains | How it is armed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CoW Protocol (keyless) | Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, BNB, Polygon, Avalanche, Linea, Ink | one exact-amount token approval (only if the allowance is short) + one gasless EIP-712 signature per order | pays the chain's native currency to your wallet; orders last 14 days; fill-or-nothing; small orders may not fill on Ethereum mainnet (gas) |
| KyberSwap limit orders (keyless) | Optimism, Unichain, Sonic, Berachain, Monad, HyperEVM, Robinhood (also available on the chains above, where CoW is preferred) | one exact-amount token approval to Kyber's order contract + one gasless EIP-712 signature per order | you receive the chain's WRAPPED coin (WETH, WBNB, ...), which you can unwrap; orders last 14 days; Kyber chooses each order's salt, so it is stored and posted back unchanged; the order's id may not be returned at creation, so it is found by matching the maker's listing |
| Jupiter trigger orders v1 (keyless on lite-api) | Solana | one transaction per order (each moves its tokens into Jupiter's escrow) | min order about $5, so small positions are merged into fewer, larger sells at the earlier target; Jupiter keeps about 0.8% (the limit is grossed up so you net the target); orders stay until filled or cancelled |

**Chains without any keyless limit-order venue: Scroll, Mantle, Blast, World Chain and Abstract** (none of CoW, Kyber or Jupiter serves them; they are also the chains that need a 0x key to swap at all). There, target sells are queued for you to sign, as before. Making them fully automatic would need a custom on-chain order contract (deployed per chain and audited) or handing the app signing power, neither of which this app does. Orders are rebuilt on the server from stored data when posted (the browser sends only ids and signatures). The monitor job books fills (`syncAutoSells`: venue state is compared with what is already booked, so partial fills and repeated syncs never double-count), records a CONFIRMED sell in the ledger, and notifies **Profit taken: SYMBOL +X%** with dollars and the overall result when a position closes. While an order covers a target the manual "sign this sell" flow skips it; uncovered targets still queue as before. Orders that expire, are cancelled elsewhere, or never land (Solana: 4 min, CoW: 10 min) are closed out with a notification.

Verified against the live services while building: CoW accepts our signed order and cancellation formats and its contracts exist on all eight chains; Kyber's order endpoint checks the signature before anything else (a wrong or garbage signature is rejected, ours passes), its cancel format is accepted once the signing domain's chainId is a number, and its listing/fill shapes come from real orders; Jupiter's createOrder works keyless and the history/fill shapes used for booking were taken from real accounts. Not exercised against a real fill of our own orders (that needs a funded wallet), and Jupiter's cancelOrder success response is parsed defensively because no real order was available to cancel.
